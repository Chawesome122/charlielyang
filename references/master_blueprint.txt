Certification, reading guide, and status

Document type: methodological master blueprint, not an empirical paper and not a claim
that numerical results have been obtained. This edition reconciles the uploaded seven iterations
and is deliberately self-critical. Each specification is identified as fixed design, conditional theorem,
to be estimated, scenario-only, or unverified implementation. An attractive formula is not an
observed result; a source URL is not an extracted dataset; a repository diagram is not working software.
No status box in this document implies otherwise.
Research question. How does the exercise of asymmetric U.S.-led semiconductor controls and Chinese
rare-earth restrictions change the opponent's short-run technological or material access, induce or suppress
long-run adaptation, and thereby change the equilibrium value and use of economic coercion? When,
if ever, does rare-earth diversification cross a durable physical-resilience threshold or a genuinely self-
reinforcing economic tipping point?
Thesis. Coercion can create contemporaneous leverage because a target relies on hard-to-substitute pro-
duction capabilities. But the same coercion can change future investment, disclosure incentives, policy
support, supply-chain topology, and technological progress. The sign and size of that feedback are em-
pirical questions. With positive induced adaptation, a suﬀiciently patient coercer may optimally restrain
current pressure because pressure consumes the future value of the chokepoint. With negative induced
adaptation, no general 'self-eroding' conclusion follows; this is a meaningful falsification possibility, not
a failure of the research.
Nonnegotiable scope. The empirical chapters are genuinely separate before integration. Rare
earths: China versus physically feasible non-Chinese supply for magnet-related elements, tracked by
mine/feedstock, oxide separation, metallurgy/alloys, and finished magnets. Semiconductors: U.S.-led
and allied controls by item, use case and jurisdiction, differentiating Chinese domestic production
capability, foreign supply access, and the combined functional bottleneck. The third component
is an observable policy-opacity covariate, not a third industrial chokepoint. An independent AI-
model/adoption ecosystem game is excluded. Demand from AI-related applications may enter as an
exogenous semiconductor demand variable.
Deliverable hierarchy. D1 = clean conceptual theory and complete variable definitions; D2 = source-
and vintage-correct data architecture; D3 = project and layer estimates; D4 = properly evaluated prob-
ability forecasts; D5 = empirically disciplined dynamic game with verified numerical diagnostics; D6 =
reproducible paper. This document aims to settle D1 and specify D2–D6. It does not assert that D2–D6
have been executed. A genuine publication-ready empirical paper requires an independently checked
dataset, code, results, robustness and references. The completion audit at the end distinguishes those
tasks explicitly.

1. Theoretical foundation and falsifiable contributions

1.1 Why the two chokepoints are asymmetric

China's advantage in magnet rare earths is much more pronounced in refining and permanent-magnet
production than in raw ore extraction. The IEA's 2026 assessment reports approximate Chinese shares
of 60% of 2024 magnet-REE mining, 91% of refined output, and 94% of sintered permanent
magnets, and substantially thinner announced non-Chinese pipelines downstream than upstream [R25].
The policy object is not simply a Chinese endowment of minerals: it is a multi-stage input-processing
system with specific chemical, engineering, feedstock and qualification constraints. Announced capacity
in one stage cannot freely replace missing capacity in another. The empirical target is physically feasible
service to specified end users under a China-removal stress test, not mining tonnes or a generic supply
concentration ratio.
By contrast, U.S.-led semiconductor leverage is a technology/access network. It involves semiconductor
design and process software, lithography, deposition/etch/implant equipment, inspection and metrology,
foundry process mastery, HBM, advanced packaging, American extraterritorial rules and U.S.-person
requirements, and controls adopted independently by allies. The BIS December 2024 package, for exam-
ple, added controls on 24 categories of semiconductor-manufacturing equipment, three types of software,
and HBM; Dutch 2025 controls also expanded to some measurement and inspection equipment; Japan's
advanced equipment rules cover 23 classes [R26–R29]. A legal licensing requirement should never be
automatically coded as a complete embargo. In January 2026 BIS shifted certain advanced-chip licenses
to conditional case-by-case review [R30]. Every control variable must be time-varying, product-specific,
and as-of-date correct.
This asymmetry shapes the data and mathematics. Rare-earth resilience may be reconstructed from
project histories, conversion yields, physical capacity and feasible network throughput. Semiconductor
resilience requires target-specific performance evidence, distinct from money spent, patents filed, wafers
started or overall chip shipment values. Reciprocal-game equations are intentionally lower-dimensional

1.2 The closest existing research and genuine novelty

Farrell and Newman [R01] theorize how central nodes of international networks can become coercive as-
sets. Zou, Poncin and Bertinelli [R04] already formulate a U.S.–China rare-earth differential game. Clay-
ton, Maggiori and Schreger [R02] explicitly model the coercion/integration/security trade-off. Woods
[R03] offers a dynamic opacity/signaling framework for rare-earth policy with illustrative, not exter-
nally estimated, simulation parameters. The semiconductor firm-level literature [R05–R07] does not
unanimously establish that restrictions increase actual capabilities: R&D, patenting, learning and op-
erating substitutability may move differently. Consequently this project must not claim originality for
inventing economic coercion, mineral differential games, or uncertainty itself.
The defensible contribution is a joint empirical-theoretical chain: (i) vintage-correct probability distri-
butions of actual non-Chinese project completion and stage-feasible magnet production; (ii) distinct
estimates of immediate Chinese semiconductor access shocks and lagged indigenous adaptation, by layer;
(iii) an auditable observability-only measure of policy opacity and tests of investment delay or overreac-
tion; (iv) a reciprocal, constrained dynamic game whose state and adaptation parameters are disciplined
by, but not presumed fully identified from, the empirical chapters; and (v) forecasts and policy counter-
factuals that quantify the erosion of future leverage under explicit uncertainty.


1.3 Hypotheses, direction of evidence, and null outcomes

H1 - Rare-earth implementation. Verified finance, permitting, engineering readiness, offtake and
government support predict commissioning beyond announced nominal capacity and target date. This
is primarily a predictive result, not necessarily a causal effect.
H2 - Strategic pressure and diversification. Greater Chinese restriction exposure changes initiation,
financing, commissioning or subsequent capacity for non-Chinese projects; the sign and lag may differ
by stage and geography.
H3 - Semiconductor first impact. U.S./allied restrictions cause or predict immediate access losses
in the targeted layer, conditional on remaining foreign substitutes, stockpiles, exemptions, and shipment
adjustment.
H4 - Semiconductor induced capability. Restriction exposure changes subsequent domestic R&D,
capex, patents and independently verified functional capability. Effects may be positive, zero, or negative,
with distinct lag distributions.
H5 - Direct opacity drag. Greater observable ambiguity or regulatory nondisclosure, separately from
prices, technological uncertainty and geopolitical risk, changes industrial project progression hazards.
The positive quadratic/overreaction variant is secondary and may be rejected.
H6 - Endogenous resilience feedback. Existing operating non-Chinese projects help or fail to help
subsequent projects pass financing, technical qualification and commissioning barriers. An observed
positive interaction is not by itself a bistable phase transition; true multiplicity requires the separate
nonlinear test in Section 5.

H7 - Self-eroding leverage. Conditional on positive restriction-induced adaptation and an intertem-
poral payoff trade-off, forward-looking optimal restriction intensity may be below the equivalent myopic
optimum. This is provable in the constant-control benchmark; its generality and magnitude in the full
reciprocal game require numerical verification.
H8 - Reciprocal strategic interaction. Estimated or scenario-based contemporaneous complemen-
tarity/retaliation can increase restrictions, while greater adaptation responsiveness and strategic patience
can offset it. No theorem guarantees universal restraint in every two-player specification.


1.4 What 'tipping' is allowed to mean

We distinguish four phenomena that earlier drafts sometimes conflated. Physical adequacy thresh-
old: modeled feasible service crosses, for example, 75% or 100% of required demand; this is a first-
passage/viability event, not a bifurcation. Steady-state feasibility threshold: investment is suf-
ficient to sustain a selected normalized resilience level in a saturating ODE; still not necessarily a bi-
furcation. Dynamical tipping: a feedback parameter creates multiple steady states and an unstable
threshold, fold, or hysteresis; this needs a formally derived condition and empirical support. Network
propagation/cascade: loss of a supplier changes feasible downstream production through physical
edges or induces correlated finance decisions; cascading input shortfalls do not establish a macroeconomic
critical transition unless additional nonlinearities are demonstrated. Keep the terms visibly separate in
the manuscript.


2. Institutional history and as-of legal coding

The master legal timeline is a table of policy instruments, not a collection of timeless assertions.
Each instrument has publication, effective and suspension/replacement times; scope, licensing standard,
affected technical classification, destinations and end uses; oﬀicial-language canonical reference; and
frozen source artifact/hash. The April 4, 2025 MOFCOM/GACC Announcement No.18 covered specified
samarium-, gadolinium-, terbium-, dysprosium-, lutetium-, scandium- and yttrium-related materials and
required export licensing [R31]. November 7, 2025 Announcement No.70 suspended separately numbered
measures 55, 56, 57, 58, 61, and 62 until November 10, 2026 [R32]. The April No.18 is not on that
suspension list; treat it as operative on the research freeze unless a different superseding law is identified.
That conclusion is an explicit inference, not a blanket legal opinion. Nd and Pr are central to magnet
recipes, but the April measure cannot be summarized as a blanket prohibition of all Nd/Pr exports.
The U.S. series begins with October 2022 advanced-computing/manufacturing restrictions, continues
through October 2023 and 2024 refinements, the December 2024 controls of software, HBM and equip-
ment, subsequent 2025 changes affecting foreign-owned fabs, and January 2026 advanced-chip licensing
revisions [R26, R30]. The Netherlands' authorization requirement was expanded in April 2025, explic-
itly not a categorical ban [R28]. Japan amended equipment controls covering 23 high-performance
categories with implementation from July 2023 [R29]. The legal codebook must respect that allied
rules are national actions and do not necessarily carry the same destinations, criteria or extraterritorial
jurisdiction as BIS instruments.

3. Rare-earth supply-chain accounting: objects, geography, and units

3.1 Supply scope and critical element bookkeeping

Fixed base basket: E = {Nd, Pr, Dy, Tb}. Preserve each element's identity through the physical
network and publish element-level bottlenecks. A practical base geography B includes U.S. and agreed
aligned consuming/processing jurisdictions; the membership table is a frozen input, not a verbal as-
sertion that everything outside China is allied. Track three distinct properties: facility location,
ownership/control (including ultimate parent), and feedstock origin. A processing facility in one country
may require feedstock from another; Chinese-origin material processed in a friendly jurisdiction cannot
be counted as wholly China-independent in a China-removal scenario. Alternate geographic definitions
are published as sensitivities.
Stages: s = 0 mineral extraction/concentrate, s = 1 separated element-specific oxide, s = 2 converted
metal/alloy or magnet powder, and s = 3 finished qualified magnet. An oxide is not the same physi-
cal object as a magnet. Raw native reported quantities include ore tonnes, mixed-REE oxide tonnes,
contained Nd tonnes, mixed NdPr oxide tonnes, metal tonnes, alloy tonnes and finished magnet tonnes.
Every source observation carries native_unit and product_basis; transformations occur only through
a versioned recipe/conversion table with evidence and uncertainty. For example, an idealized Nd2 O3
mass is not one-for-one Nd metal mass; conversion follows molecular weights and actual recovery. A

only a transport/logistics provider, its affected edges are removed and nodes remain when appropriate.

silently classify mixed-control sites.

6.5 Two-step empirical program

Step A: immediate access effect. Define firm-by-product/month and destination-layer panels from
oﬀicial customs/trade, firm financials, regulation/event mappings and supplier reports; outcome variables
can include log(1+quantity), real unit value, inventory, non-coalition sourcing share, equipment service
continuity, and well-documented legal license approvals. Use exposure frozen pre-policy and distinguish
publication from implementation. The pre-registered estimation estimand is group-time ATT for first
treatment Gi = g, ATT(g, t) = E[Yt (1) − Yt (0) | Gi = g], under conditional parallel trends with
matched or not-yet-treated controls, if the treatment design supports it. Estimate cohort-time effects
with Callaway–Sant'Anna or an appropriate Sun–Abraham implementation, not a naive heterogeneous-
treatment TWFE lead/lag alone [R14–R15]. Cluster by treatment assignment (firm parent/sector where
appropriate), allow event clustering, and use wild/bootstrap or randomization checks if few treated
clusters. Plot pretrends, leads, overlap and attrition. If exposure is continuous, consider dose-specific
designs [R16] rather than interpreting an ordinal 0/0.25/… index as exact physical dosage.
Step B: delayed domestic adaptation. Outcomes are separately R&D spending, capex, training,
quality-adjusted patent families, qualified tool substitution, process yields, and evidence-coded capability
z. The effects may have different signs and lags; e.g. spending can rise as actual frontier capability stalls.
Pre-register windows of 0–6, 6–12, 12–24 and >24 months, subject to realized observability. A positive
effect on R&D is not a positive effect on z. Model dynamic capability levels using the bounded state
law and a measurement equation for the coarse 0/0.25/… ratings with coder uncertainty. If capability
data are too thin, do not pretend to identify ηℓ structurally: report estimated effort responses, observed
milestones, and scenario intervals for the conversion into functional capability.
Threats to causal interpretation: selection into Entity List treatment; treatment anticipation; simul-
taneous Chinese industrial subsidies; COVID/global cyclical equipment shortages; direct effect of U.S.
rules on American supplier revenue and R&D; policy spillovers to ostensibly untreated Chinese firms;
heterogeneous treatment timing; globally moving technology frontier; record-based selection; and non-
equivalent HS/technology crosswalk. Mitigation requires contemporaneous controls, event-time placebos,
technology negative-control groups, exposure perturbations, sensitivity to Chinese subsidy announce-
ments, and candid limits on causal language.

8.6 HJB numerical method, algorithm and admissibility diagnostics

A candidate pure feedback MPE is obtained iteratively, not just by solving nine algebraic regimes at one
state. A reproducible deterministic base algorithm is:

1. Primitive registry. Freeze g, β, δ, κ, ρ, A, D, k, h, B, q with estimation/calibration/scenario labels.
   Reject impossible input domains before solver start.
2. Tensor state grid. Start Nx = Ny = Nm = Nn = 17 (83,521 nodes), then 21 (194,481 nodes) and
   31 (923,521 nodes) only if memory/time feasibility demonstrated; record sparse matrix sizes
   and measured runtime/memory, not assume a workstation handles a 31^4 nonlinear solve cheaply.
3. Initial policy pairs. Start from zero/zero, one/one, one/zero, zero/one, midpoint/midpoint and
   seeded random smooth policies. A single convergence path cannot establish uniqueness.
4. Policy evaluation. Holding σU , σC fixed, solve two separate sparse discounted linear equations
   (ρi I −Lσ )Vi = πiσ . The generator Lσ uses dimension-wise upwind first differences: forward derivative
   when state drift is positive, backward when negative, state-constrained inward stencils at endpoints.
   The diagonal discount term ensures a proper monotone discounted discretization under admissibility.
5. Policy improvement. Derive VU,m and VC,n from grid-consistent one-sided differences; compute
   MU , MC , enumerate all nine KKT regimes at each grid point, record every distinct candidate, and
   update σ with damping/line search if the joint step oscillates. Re-evaluate until fixed point.

 Weaponizing Interdependence


 6. Residual. Define Ri = ρi Vi − {πi (s, σ) + Lσ Vi } as the discrete continuous-time HJB
    residual (units utility/year), and separately compute each unilateral deviation advantage
    GU = maxu∈[0,1] HU (s, u, σC ; VU ) − HU (s, σU , σC ; VU ), analogously for China. Do not write an
    undefined ∥B(V ) − V ∥ without defining B.
 7. Acceptance thresholds. Scale numerical residual tolerances by the payoff magnitude, e.g. ∥Ri ∥∞ ≤
    10−8 (1 + ∥πi ∥∞ ) and action KKT ≤ 10−7 at the selected mesh if precision permits, plus grid-
    refinement changes and held-out-policy verification. A small residual on a coarse grid is not proof of
    the exact continuous solution.
 8. Verification and equilibrium multiplicity. Starting from each initial pair, classify distinct poli-
    cies by sup norm and economically relevant policy decisions; if no pure candidate converges, refine,
    change damping or use a finite-state/action approximation and compute a stationary mixed Markov
    equilibrium under suitable discounted-game assumptions. Fink [R20] provides the existence basis

    MPE.
 9. Closed-loop dynamics. Form f (s, σU (s), σC (s)), solve stationary points and compute its full Jaco-

10.2 Historical evidence protocol: two clocks plus source revisions

Every table has at least valid_date and info_public_date. Example: a facility starts commercial pro-
duction on 2023-09-01; that fact is first publicly documented 2024-02-10. The full-information analytical
panel may use September as the retrospectively observed event date, but a December 2023 historical
forecast must not contain that fact. It must treat the project as unresolved as of December 2023.
Any observed revision is retained as a new record_version_id with its own source hash/publication date;
the earlier record stays immutable. If the earliest public date is unknown, exclude it from the relevant
vintage forecast or conservatively place it after the earliest verifiable availability date and quantify the
resulting loss of sample.
